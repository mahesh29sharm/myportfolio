# Exhibition Plan

## Curatorial thesis

This portfolio should feel like entering one maker's private archive after hours: part exhibition, part evidence room, part sketchbook. The recurring thread is not a discipline or job title. It is the instinct to turn an idea into a world people can enter—through a story, symbol, poster, restaurant voice, publication, or product.

The work is not equal in scale, and the site should not pretend that it is. The best pieces get silence, width, and time. Related fragments gather into walls, trays, and sequences. Professional commissions, self-initiated experiments, unpublished explorations, and published work are always labeled honestly.

## Recommended collection order

### 1. Visual Stories

**Story:** Ideas become memorable when information is given atmosphere. This room introduces the archive through four continuous Udgamreads narratives: myth, science, character, and lived time.

**Assets:** All four files in `Carousel- visual storytelling/`.

**Presentation:** Begin with `Arcane 1.png` as a near-cinematic horizontal scroll. Let each remaining strip occupy a full browser-width stage with a short human caption and optional “read the full strip” mode. Preserve the continuous compositions. Avoid carousel UI dots or card chrome. Close with `image 95.png`, whose reflective tone creates a natural bridge from public work to personal voice.

**Label:** Self-initiated/editorial work, pending confirmation. Keep the visible Udgamreads credit intact.

### 2. Imaginary Worlds

**Story:** Portraiture becomes world-building. Familiar figures are reconstructed through color, atmosphere, and the symbols surrounding them.

**Assets:** `Einstein_Poster 1.png`, `Oppie_Final copy 2 1.png`, `batman9 1.png`, and the three `Folder*.png` mockups. Cross-link to the Oppenheimer visual story without duplicating it in the main sequence.

**Presentation:** A monumental poster triptych: each original poster should be at least one viewport tall. Use the mockups as smaller archival objects on a shared “table” beneath the posters. Alternate dark, red, and yellow room tones derived from the artwork rather than placing everything on one neutral grid.

**Label:** Personal exploration unless a commission is later confirmed.

### 3. Brands in the Wild

**Story:** Brand expression is not only a logo. It is the way a restaurant sketches a dish, explains a sauce, announces a meal, or speaks in the language of its city.

**Assets:** All Fervor and SuperNaati files. Add Fava and BLR Bistro only when real assets and credits are supplied.

**Presentation:** Split the room into two distinct environments.

- **Fervor — From kitchen notes to communication:** Start with the raw sketch, move through the notebook and plated illustration, then resolve into the three green ingredient diagrams. Use a long workbench layout.
- **SuperNaati — A brand with an appetite and an accent:** Treat Lunch Language as a six-poster street wall; present Ghee Roast as a three-beat story; let Beegara Oota and Egg Curry behave like changing menu-board announcements.

Do not flatten these into matched cards. Fervor is quiet and process-led; SuperNaati is loud, cultural, and campaign-led.

**Label:** Client/commercial work, but confirm exact role and collaborators before publication. Do not imply creation of either master brand identity from the present evidence.

### 4. Marks & Meaning

**Story:** A symbol can carry an entire promise before it becomes a logo. This room shows the act of compressing ideas—growth, fitness, community, prosperity, exploration—into elemental forms.

**Assets:** All seven files in `Symbols/`.

**Presentation:** A sparse specimen room. Place the terracotta glyphs at radically different scales, with their concept words functioning like museum labels. Add one “constellation” view showing the full family together. If sketches arrive later, reveal them on hover or through a process drawer.

**Required disclosure:** “Exploratory symbol directions for Ultrahuman, created through Yaas Media. Not selected or produced.” Confirm wording and credits before launch.

### 5. Things I Built

**Story:** Some ideas need to become places, not images. The product chapter should be framed around the impulse to make spaces for expression and participation.

**Assets:** The eight Trace screens. Articled should be added only when its assets are located or when Trace is confirmed to be the same product.

**Presentation:** Show the mobile screens as one readable journey—discover, enter, respond, react, continue, write—rather than a UI gallery. Allow the screens to overlap like a live conversation. A brief first-person note can carry the motivation; no conventional problem/process/solution case-study scaffold is needed.

**Editorial caution:** The supplied origin story—wanting somewhere to express opinions without worrying about judgment—belongs to Articled. Do not attach it to Trace until their relationship is confirmed. Replace the duplicate `topic-detail.png` export.

### 6. Stories That Travel

**Story:** A story changes when it leaves the screen, becomes an object, and enters public life. The Modi 101 graphic novel is shown here as a published artifact with a documented afterlife.

**Assets:** All four files in `Modi 101/`; future cover, interior spreads, ISBN/publication data, launch photographs, and press evidence.

**Presentation:** The current photographs form an evidence wall—small, documentary, captioned with names, places, and dates only after verification. The eventual hero should be the book itself: cover, binding, and interior sequences photographed as an object. A horizontal “travel log” can record publication and circulation without claiming unverified scale or endorsement.

**Label:** Published storytelling work. Add exact role, collaborators, publisher, year, language editions, and any verified reach.

### 7. Building a Brand From Zero — Lucio

**Story:** Founding design is the work of giving a young company a visual center before the rest of the system exists.

**Assets:** All six Lucio files, led by `Construction Banner Mockup.png` and `Tote Bag.png`.

**Presentation:** Open with the construction banner at architectural scale. Follow with a restrained identity specimen—mark, wordmark, color, typography—then the tote as an everyday object. The room should feel precise and spacious, using Lucio’s gradient as ambient light rather than a decorative background everywhere.

**Suggested copy:** “I was Lucio’s founding designer, shaping its early identity and first creative assets. The company later reported a $5 million fundraise. These facts belong to the same timeline; one should not be presented as the cause of the other.” Replace “reported” with a linked primary source before launch, or omit the amount.

### 8. Behind the Work

**Story:** The archive ends with the person who notices things: night streets, toy figures, changing skies, a crowded desk, a train doorway. This is not a formal About page disguised as a moodboard; it is the visual evidence of a particular eye.

**Assets:** The eight images in `me/`.

**Presentation:** Use the train portrait as the human anchor and the desk as the creative habitat. Arrange the night scene, LEGO figures, and skies as an irregular contact strip with handwritten-length captions: one observation each, not mini essays. Use only one of the near-duplicate pink sunsets unless their sequence has a specific story.

**Closing thought:** “I’ve been making and building things since 2016. The mediums keep changing. The instinct doesn’t.”

## Exhibition-wide rules

- Use three honest labels throughout: **Commissioned**, **Self-initiated**, and **Exploratory / unselected**. Add **Published** where relevant.
- Give every collection a different spatial behavior; do not reuse a project-card component as the dominant pattern.
- Keep captions short: title, year, role, context, collaborators, and one sentence of intent.
- Let a few works remain unexplained on first view. The imagery should earn curiosity before the metadata answers it.
- Preserve original aspect ratios and sequence order. Never crop a continuous carousel or poster merely to satisfy a grid.
- Use cross-links where themes recur, especially Oppenheimer, rather than repeating the same asset as if it were separate work.
- Avoid claims about business impact, audience size, endorsement, adoption, or authorship unless documented.

## Strongest homepage opening statement

> **I make stories, symbols and systems. This is where I keep the evidence.**

Supporting line:

> A personal creative archive of imagined worlds, living brands, published stories, and things I’ve built since 2016.
